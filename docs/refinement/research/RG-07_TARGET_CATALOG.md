## 1. The question

How can choosing a known object never require looking RA and Dec up elsewhere: which names and
identifiers search should find; whether and how the catalog should grow (sources, licences, size on
the device, the candidates' evaluation time); an offline catalog or an online name resolver; and when
custom entry is still needed (RG-07; 08 §4, §9).

## 2. What the app has today [verified]

- **164 objects**, generated by `tool/build_catalog.dart` from OpenNGC v20260501 (CC BY-SA 4.0, with
  `OPENNGC_NOTICE.txt` and the About page): the 109 Messier objects present in OpenNGC and 55
  owner-approved showpieces (TASK 8.2).
- **Stored per object:** the display id ("M31", "NGC 7000"), the first common name, type, J2000
  coordinates, major axis and V magnitude. The asset also carries OpenNGC's own name (`openNgc`, for
  example "NGC0224" for M31), which the seeder does not store.
- **Search** is `LIKE %query%` on the id and the common name: "Andromeda" finds M31, but "M 31",
  "Messier 31", "NGC7000", "NGC 0224" or "NGC 224" (M31's NGC number) find nothing.
- **Seeding** adds the entries of a newer catalog version (`since`) and never updates an existing row,
  so deleted targets do not come back and a user's edit of a catalog target's name is kept.
- **Custom targets** need a name, RA and Dec (J2000; sexagesimal or decimal, parsed by `AstroMath`);
  type, size and magnitude are optional.
- **"What can I image tonight?"** evaluates every stored target; with 164 targets it takes about
  **95 ms** on the desktop test VM (TASK 15.2's benchmark, `TEST_PLAN.md`), in a background isolate.

## 3. What the pinned OpenNGC release holds [verified, counted from v20260501]

- `NGC.csv` has 13,969 rows and `addendum.csv` 64; **13,371 objects** once duplicates (`Dup`, 652) and
  non-existent entries (`NonEx`, 10) are left out. Most are galaxies (`G`, 10,521); then open clusters
  (663), stars and double stars (790), `Other` (419), galaxy pairs and groups (270), globular clusters
  (208), planetary nebulae (130), and emission, reflection and HII nebulae, clusters with nebulosity and
  supernova remnants (about 300 together).
- **Common names are rare: only 151 objects have one.** Every catalog object without a name (97)
  has none upstream either, so **OpenNGC cannot name them.** 16 catalog objects have more than one
  common name upstream, and the build keeps only the first.
- **Cross-identifiers** (`Identifiers`, and the `M`, `NGC`, `IC` columns [primary: OpenNGC's
  `NGC_guide.txt`]): 12,234 objects have some. Useful for search: the Messier number (`M`), the NGC/IC
  designation of every Messier object, **Caldwell numbers** (105 objects, plus Caldwell entries in the
  addendum), **LBN** numbers (94), Melotte (addendum). Mostly they are survey ids (PGC, 2MASX, SDSS,
  MCG, UGC, IRAS…) that imagers do not search by.
- **Missing from OpenNGC:** Sharpless nebulae (one "SH" identifier in the whole file), van den Bergh
  reflection nebulae, most Barnard dark nebulae (B 33 only, in the addendum), Abell planetary nebulae
  by Abell number. OpenNGC is an NGC/IC catalog by design [primary: its README: the addendum holds
  "objects that are not part of the NGC or IC catalog", a small set].
- **Brightness subsets** (non-duplicates with a V magnitude): 542 at V ≤ 10, 899 at V ≤ 11, 1,754 at
  V ≤ 12, 2,914 at V ≤ 13. 237 objects have a major axis of 10′ or more. Many nebulae have no V
  magnitude, so a magnitude cut alone would drop them [verified: blank fields].
- **Licence:** CC BY-SA 4.0, "which allows the use for a wider range of cases"; upstream credits NED,
  HyperLEDA, SIMBAD, HEASARC and Corwin [primary: OpenNGC README]. Already accepted for the app (TASK
  8.2; PD-12); its compatibility with the app's licence is RG-12's question in Stage 9.

## 4. Other sources

- **CDS (SIMBAD, VizieR)** [primary: CDS legal page]: datasets are shared under "an Open Licence or
  ODbL … or CC-BY", **per dataset**, and must be cited "including … the DOI … as well as the source by
  mentioning the CDS service" ("CDS/SIMBAD", "CDS/VizieR"). The Sharpless catalogue is in VizieR
  [unverified: catalogue number and its own licence not read]. A bundled extract would need that
  dataset's licence read and matched against CC BY-SA and GPL-3.0 (RG-12) [unknown today].
- **An online resolver** — CDS **Sesame** "queries several databases from the name of an astronomical
  object (outside the Solar System bodies)": SIMBAD, NED and VizieR, returning the type and J2000
  position [primary: the Sesame page, through a search result]. What would leave the device: the typed
  name and the device's IP address. Its rate limits and availability guarantees were not found
  [unknown]. Its terms are CDS's (the citation above) [primary].
- **Scraping** Wikipedia, Telescopius or similar lists is rejected (PD-05; `PRODUCT_DIRECTION.md` §6).

## 5. Size and time

- **The asset:** about 258 bytes of JSON per object today (42,366 bytes for 164) [verified]. The whole
  of OpenNGC would be about 3.5 MB uncompressed; V ≤ 11 about 230 KB [estimates from that ratio].
- **The database:** one row per object, seeded on first run or on a catalog update; the seeding time
  for thousands of rows on a phone is [unknown] (Stage 10 measures).
- **"What can I image tonight?":** about 0.58 ms per target on the desktop VM (95 ms / 164). Linear
  estimates: ~0.5 s for 900 targets, ~1 s for 1,750, ~7.7 s for 13,400 — on a desktop; a phone is
  slower [unverified by how much]. It also changes what the list means: thousands of faint galaxies
  would bury the showpieces the list is for, unless it is filtered.
- **FACT:** growth has a real cost in the candidates list; search alone does not.

## 6. The alias and search rules (testable examples)

A match ignores case, spaces, hyphens and leading zeros in a designation's number, and matches a common
name by substring as today:

| Typed | Finds | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "M31", "M 31", "m31", "Messier 31" | M31 | the id; "Messier" as a synonym of "M" |
| "NGC 224", "NGC224", "NGC 0224" | M31 | OpenNGC's own name for M31 (`openNgc`, already in the asset) |
| "Andromeda" | M31 | the common name (works today) |
| "NGC7000", "ngc 7000" | NGC 7000 | the id |
| "C 20", "C020", "Caldwell 20" | NGC 7000 | OpenNGC `Identifiers` (verified: "C 020,LBN 373" on NGC 7000) |
| "LBN 25" | M8 (Lagoon Nebula) | OpenNGC `Identifiers` (verified: "LBN 25" on M8) |
| a second common name (16 objects) | that object | OpenNGC `Common names` |
| "Sh2-129" | nothing offline | not in OpenNGC (§3); only an online resolver or a custom target |

- **Suggestions:** as-you-type results ordered by how the query matches (exact designation, then a
  designation prefix, then a name substring), then by the id. **No score** and no popularity order
  (ADR-013's rule applies to lists too).
- **FACT:** everything in this table except the last row comes from data the app already bundles or
  from the same pinned release.

## 7. Custom entry

- **FACT:** still needed for anything no bundled source holds (Sharpless, vdB, most Barnard and Abell
  objects, a user's own field), unless an online resolver is adopted.
- **FACT:** it must keep asking for the name, RA and Dec; pasting a sexagesimal position already works.
  With a resolver, a found position would be a proposal with its source ("CDS/SIMBAD"), confirmed by
  the user and stored as a custom target with that provenance; offline, the form works as today.

## 8. Options and recommendations

- **T1 — names and aliases, no new objects:** store the aliases of §6 for the bundled objects (the
  NGC/IC designation of each Messier object, every OpenNGC common name, Caldwell and LBN numbers), and
  normalise the search. Same source and licence; offline; no change to the candidates list.
  - **Mechanism (IMPLEMENTATION OPTION):** aliases in their own table keyed by the catalog id, rebuilt
    from the asset whenever the catalog version rises (a schema change and a catalog version bump).
    This gives existing installs the aliases without touching a row the user may have edited, and a
    deleted target's aliases are simply not shown.
- **T2 — T1 and more OpenNGC objects:** a bounded subset generated by the tool (for example the
  nebulae, clusters and bright galaxies up to a magnitude, with every nebula kept whatever its
  magnitude). Same source and licence. Costs: the candidates list's time and noise (§5).
- **T3 — T2 and another bundled catalog** (for example Sharpless): only after its own licence and
  provenance are read and accepted; unknown today.
- **T4 — an optional online lookup** in the custom-target form ("Look up online"): Sesame by name, the
  result a proposal with its source, confirmed by the user, stored as a custom target; never automatic,
  never needed offline. The privacy policy and compliance documents name CDS (trap 22); the user agent
  is the app's.

**Recommend T1** (FACT: it fixes every failing example of §6 but one from data already bundled, with no
new licence, no network and no effect on the candidates list; the owner's complaint in 08 §4 and §9 is
about finding known objects by name).

**T2** is an OWNER PREFERENCE: it adds objects most users would not choose, and costs the candidates
list. If wanted, a small curated addition (named nebulae and clusters) is safer than a magnitude cut.

**T3 is not recommended now:** no dataset's licence was read.

**T4** is an OWNER PREFERENCE with a privacy trade-off: it is the only option that finds Sharpless and
similar objects without typing coordinates. Its rate limits and availability are unknown, so it must
fail gracefully (the form stays usable). Recommended only if the owner accepts one more online service,
opt-in like the place names (PD-12) or on each tap.

## 9. The owner's questions (RG-07)

1. **Names and aliases:** T1 (aliases from the pinned OpenNGC and normalised search, in their own table;
   recommended)?
2. **More objects:** none (recommended), a curated set of named nebulae and clusters from OpenNGC, or a
   magnitude-bounded subset (which limit)?
3. **An online lookup:** no; or T4 (Sesame, a proposal the user confirms), and if so, opt-in in Settings
   (like place names) or simply on each tap of "Look up online"?
